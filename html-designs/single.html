Why Every Tech Event Needs Pre-Conference Feedback Sessions

Speakers testing their presentations on stage for the first time. The result? Stressed speakers and disappointed attendees. Here's what to do instead.

After organizing and speaking at dozens of tech conferences, I've noticed a recurring problem: speakers testing their presentations on stage for the first time. The result? Stressed speakers and disappointed attendees. At WebExpo Prague, I discovered a solution that changed everything — feedback sessions.

What are feedback sessions?

It's a very simple idea — you gather in groups of 3–5 people (including a person who leads such a session), and everyone presents their talk. Afterwards, everyone (including the speaker) lists what they liked and what can be improved.

Because those sessions happen at least a week before the event, people have a lot of time to improve their talks based on the feedback.

Why did I want to have them at WordCamp Gdynia?

Let's be honest — all speakers know their topic very well. But they fall short in other departments:

  • Not practicing enough before the event
  • Preparing slides at the last moment, resulting in a lack of polish
  • Putting too much text on the slides

Feedback sessions give you an early deadline, which means you'll start working on your talk earlier. And I know how stressful public speaking can be — especially for first-timers. This gives you a dry run in front of people in a safe environment.

How did it work in Gdynia?

Really well. All presentations were strong on content. In most cases, we had to polish some details.

Slide layout

The most popular mistake — too much text on slides. Slides should only be a roadmap of your talk, not the full scenario. People come to listen to you, not to read slides.

Order and flow

Every story should start with a strong statement. Very often those statements were buried later in the slides. We had to move things around to make the flow more natural and interesting.

Removing useless stuff

In Gdynia, speakers had 20 minutes. Removing every unnecessary element was crucial — intro slides, tangents, example videos that ran too long. Out.

What you need to know before running feedback sessions

  • Each session took 40–45 minutes per speaker — a significant time commitment
  • Be empathic but honest — encourage starting with positives before the constructive critique
  • Stay focused — people are using their free time, take notes and listen

Maciek created a safe space for people preparing to speak. Feedback between speakers, exchange of ideas, and sharing tips increased confidence and often resulted in positive changes to presentation plans.

— Magda Ślęzak, WordCamp Wrocław organizer

Afterwards, I did a survey — 100% of participants said those sessions were very useful. All speakers who attended got really good feedback from attendees too.
